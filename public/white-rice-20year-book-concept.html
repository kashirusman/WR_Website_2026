Reading the 15-Year Book First

What worked, and what the 20-year book must do differently.

The 15-year coffee table book is a warm, earnest document — a proud institutional record of a company that had every right to celebrate. The photography was genuine, the statistics real, and the commitment to impact unmistakeable. It did exactly what a first-edition book should: establish that White Rice existed, had done remarkable things, and had the numbers to prove it.

But the 20-year book arrives in a different moment. White Rice is no longer a promising agency proving itself — it is an established intellectual force in South Asia's development landscape. UNICEF. World Bank. Johns Hopkins CCP. Cannes Lions. The CRICKET Playbook. Programmes touching 250 million lives. That demands a different register entirely.

The 20-year book must be something a UNICEF country director leaves on their office table not just to show clients, but because it genuinely moves them every time they open it.

15-Year Book · 2021
Agency Credibility Document
Statistics, process diagrams, photo collages, testimonials. Proves White Rice exists and delivers. A portfolio showcase structured as a celebration.
20-Year Book · 2026
A Work of Visual Journalism
A document that earns its place alongside the best SBC thinking in the world. An intellectual and emotional statement that makes people understand behaviour change through White Rice's lens.

"The 15-year book tells people White Rice exists. The 20-year book must make them feel the work — and understand why it matters more than almost anything else being done in this space."

The gap between the two books is not five years. It is Cannes Lions. It is the CRICKET Playbook. It is the World Bank's 2,000 villages. It is Walidain HumQadam's 550 vision canvases — Pakistan's largest. It is the Khawaja Sira community hearing their own voices in an animation series for the first time. It is a community health worker with no background in behavioural science picking up a card from a deck and designing an intervention that works.

The 20-year book must hold all of that — with the visual sophistication and intellectual confidence the work deserves. Not a report. Not a portfolio. A book that people who have never heard of White Rice will read cover to cover, and finish with a clear understanding of what behaviour change actually is, why it matters, and why White Rice has spent twenty years working in that territory.

The Central Concept · Book Title

The Space
Between.

Twenty Years of Social Behaviour Change · White Rice Impact · 2006–2026

White Rice has always worked in a specific, under-recognised territory: the distance between what people know and what people do. Between a government building a latrine and a community using it. Between a mother learning about nutrition and a child no longer stunting. Between a girl knowing she has rights and a father believing it. That distance — where knowledge becomes habit, where information becomes life — is exactly where behaviour change happens. And it is exactly where White Rice has worked for twenty years. The book does not tell the story of a company. It tells the story of that space — and the humans who inhabit it.

Design Philosophy · Three Principles

Not a portfolio. Not a report. A book.

01
Editorial, Not Institutional
The visual language draws from the world's best editorial publishing — Monocle, National Geographic, IDEO Field Guide — not from NGO annual reports or agency credentials decks. Every spread should feel like it was made by a publisher who also cared about development, not a development agency that also hired a designer. Photography is consistently graded, full-bleed. Typography is generous, considered, never crowded. White space is as important as what fills it.
02
People Before Projects
The 15-year book showed projects. The 20-year book shows people. Not as subjects of intervention, but as protagonists of their own change. The mother in Sindh who attended a story circle and found words for what she already knew. The father in Battagram who discovered he wanted to be more present for his daughter. The frontline worker who, using a deck of cards, designed a behaviour change intervention herself. Every chapter opens with a face, not a logo. Verbatims before statistics. The numbers, when they come, are earned.
03
Bilingual by Design
Pakistan's languages are treated as design elements, not translations. Urdu and Punjabi script runs as ghost typography behind English headers. Pashto verse becomes visual texture on a Battagram spread. The CRICKET acronym in Urdu letters — ک ر ک ٹ — becomes a typographic hero, the centrepiece of one of the book's most striking spreads. The book looks like it was made in South Asia, for the world — not in London, for South Asia. This is the single most distinctive thing the 20-year book can do that the 15-year book did not.
Book Architecture · Seven Chapters

Organised by human tension,
not by project category.

I
Origin

A Bowl of Rice & a Chinese Restaurant

~20 pages · Narrative · Archive & portrait photography

The founding story retold with the depth it has now earned. Two men at a restaurant. A name chosen because white rice is a canvas — it becomes something different with every ingredient. Khawar's vision and legacy, five years deeper into its impact. Raheel's evolution from designer to behaviour scientist. Told as a dual portrait through personal photography, handwritten notes, and archive images. Ends with the question that has driven everything since: "What does it actually take for a person to change?"

Signature Spreads
Full-bleed: Raheel at a whiteboard, back to camera, facing a wall of questions. Opposite: the founding story in a single serif paragraph, 80pt leading, generous margins. Nothing else.
In Memoriam: Khawar's portrait — full-page, black and white, no caption. Opposite page: his name, the years, and a single quote about transformation. That is all.
The timeline: 2006–2026 as a single horizontal line across a double-page spread. Milestones as typographic moments, not icons or bubbles.
II
The Theory

Why Knowing Is Never Enough

~16 pages · Infographic · Intellectual framework

The intellectual core — a beautifully designed, accessible explanation of why behaviour change is the hardest and most important thing in development. The socio-ecological model, B=MAP, the CRICKET framework, the four-stage process — all rendered as visual art, not slide decks. Inspired by the infographic design of Edward Tufte and the intellectual clarity of the best science communicators: information made beautiful because it respects the reader's intelligence. The chapter that positions White Rice as the intellectual leader it has become.

Signature Spreads
Double-page: The distance between knowledge and behaviour rendered as a topographic map. The terrain is the psychosocial landscape between knowing and doing.
CRICKET: the four Urdu letters — ک ر ک ٹ — as architectural heroes at 300pt, each letter opening into its English meaning. The most striking intellectual spread in the book.
B=MAP as a mathematical proof: near-black background, the formula in serif at 200pt. Opposite: what it means in a specific village in Sindh.
III
Twenty Stories

Twenty Years. Twenty Conversations.

~80 pages · Portfolio · Colour-coded by project

The book's visual centrepiece — twenty projects, each given a 4-page spread. Not case studies: each is told as a conversation between White Rice and the community. Left page: the challenge, the place, the people, in the most arresting photography possible. Right page: what happened, told with the restraint of good journalism. Each project carries its own dominant colour from the existing case study identity system. The cumulative effect is a colour journey through twenty years of work.

Signature Spreads
Alif Ailaan: A child's hand holding a slate in a dust-filled classroom. Opposite: the words "educational emergency" in Urdu at full-page scale — the phrase White Rice named, that reached the Prime Minister's address.
Sightsavers/Cannes: The lion trophy, half in shadow, half lit — gold light on community faces. A film award shown through the people it was made for.
UNFPA Transgender: Community members alongside the animation characters drawn from their real lives. A spread about who gets to see themselves represented.
IV
The Faces

The People Who Changed

~24 pages · Full-bleed portraiture · Verbatims only

Sixteen pages of full-bleed portrait photography. No captions. No project names. No statistics. Just faces — the mother, the frontline worker, the father, the girl. Between portraits: a single verbatim in a large serif face, the person's own words about what changed, in Urdu with English translation. This is the most emotionally powerful section. It is the anti-impact-report — it refuses to quantify the humans at the centre of everything. The reader reaches it already convinced, and this chapter stays with them longest.

Signature Spreads
Full-bleed: A Sindhi mother looking directly at camera. No caption. Opposite: a verbatim in Urdu, English beneath at smaller scale, in Cormorant italic. The silence between them is part of the spread.
The Battagram father: what it felt like the first time he played with his daughter because someone asked him to. One paragraph. One face. One spread.
V
The Numbers

Impact as Typography

~12 pages · Data as visual art · Single stat per spread

The impact chapter — but radical in approach. Each number is given a full page or double-page, rendered at a scale that makes it architectural. "34,000,000" fills an entire spread at 400pt, near-black on near-white. Below it, in small DM Sans: "people reached through digital platforms for Clean & Green Punjab alone." Turning from one giant number to the next is visceral. By the end, the reader has a felt sense of scale that statistics in a table could never produce. The chapter is completely silent except for the numbers and one sentence per spread.

Signature Spreads
"11,000" — double-page, vermillion on night-black. Beneath: "Mothers. Story circles. Sindh. The world's largest storytelling exercise ever conducted." Four words. One sentence. That is all.
"250,000" in ghost grey — beneath: "frontline workers trained globally to give a child a vaccine." Johns Hopkins CCP. Six episodes. One handshake across continents.
VI
The Ecosystem

The Partners Who Believed First

~16 pages · Testimonials · Network map

Not an acknowledgements list — a portrait of a network. The chapter opens with a double-page constellation map: every partner, client, and collaborator in the White Rice ecosystem, rendered as nodes of light on a dark ground. Lines of connection. Clusters by geography and theme. Then: six partner testimonials, each given a full spread — the partner's face, their words, the project they are speaking about. The difference between this and the 15-year book's testimonials: here, we show the relationship and the specific moment of transformation it produced.

Signature Spreads
The constellation map: 100+ organisations as light-points on deep space. Drawn from Karachi outward, the map traces 20 years of trust built one project at a time.
UNICEF spread: the most powerful verbatim offered about the stunting programme — about the story circles, about the scale of what was built in Sindh, about what it means to see science become life.
VII
The Manifesto

The Next Twenty: A Declaration

~12 pages · Essay · Raheel's voice · Direct

The closing chapter is not "The Future." It is a manifesto. Raheel's voice, direct and confident. What White Rice has learned about the science of change in twenty years. What it intends to do with that knowledge in the next twenty. Written not as a letter to "Valued Partners" but to the people whose behaviours White Rice is still working to change — the farmers, mothers, girls, frontline workers. The chapter ends with a single sentence, full-page, in a font size that cannot be ignored: the reason White Rice exists.

Signature Spreads
The opener: Raheel's signature — not his headshot — full-page. The manifesto begins on the opposite page in 14pt Cormorant with generous leading. Intimate. No corporate distance.
Closing spread: A single question, full double-page, at 120pt — "How many more lives are waiting in the space between?" Then silence. The back cover.

Design Rationale · Spread 02
Why vermillion · why Urdu first

The CRICKET Playbook is White Rice's most original intellectual contribution to the SBC field globally. It deserves the most energetic spread in the book — the one that makes someone stop, hold the book up, and call someone over to look.

A reader who speaks Urdu will feel, perhaps for the first time in a development publication, that their language is the primary one. Every other reader gets a glimpse of what it is to navigate a world in someone else's alphabet. That moment of disorientation — brief, safe, and immediately resolved by the facing page — is the most valuable thing this spread can do.

Design Rationale · Spread 04
The award spread philosophy

Awards appear once.
Earned by the story,
not displayed as trophies.

The 20-year book has Cannes Lions, Australian High Commission awards, PASHA recognition, and more. None of them get a dedicated awards page. They appear within the project spreads that earned them — as a consequence, as a footnote, as a single gold line at the base of the relevant spread. This is the discipline of a book that is confident it does not need to boast.

Design System · The Visual Language

A book with a distinct
identity of its own.

Photography Direction
Field-First. Full-Bleed. No Stock.
Every photograph from White Rice's field archives or newly commissioned. All photography goes through a consistent colour treatment: warm shadow base (deep amber underlay), clean highlights, slightly desaturated midtones — simultaneously documentary and editorial. No studio photography except for partner portraits. Every full-bleed image bled to the physical edge of the page with no margins. Photography sections are printed on uncoated 170gsm — the warm, textured feel of a field notebook, not a glossy brochure.
Paper & Production
Three Paper Stocks. One Book.
Three stocks act as chapter signals: uncoated 170gsm for field photography sections (Chapters III and IV), art-coated 150gsm for the campaign and creative work (project spreads), slightly tinted 120gsm Munken Lynx for the intellectual and text chapters (I, II, VII). The transition between stocks is the book's physical signal that you are entering a different register. Chapter separators: deep terracotta card inserts, one per chapter, with the chapter title in blind emboss.
Format & Cover
340 × 270mm Landscape. Cloth. Die-Cut.
Landscape format — wider than tall — allows full-bleed photography to breathe horizontally. The format of the great documentary photography books, not the corporate brochure. Cover: cloth-wrapped boards with a die-cut window revealing the first internal page — a community portrait bleeding through the opening. Title foil-stamped in warm gold on the spine only. The front cover is intentionally minimal: cloth texture, a window, and the weight of what is inside.

Deliberately reduced from the case study system — the book is a unified editorial object, not a rainbow of project colours. Five Years Added · What the 20-Year Book Can Claim

Since the last book, White Rice earned
the right to say something entirely different.

New · 2023
Cannes Lions — Health & Wellness Category
The Sightsavers film. Pakistan's first Cannes Lion in health communication. The 15-year book had six domestic and regional awards. The 20-year book has one international award that changes the level of every conversation with a global partner who picks it up.
New · 2024
The CRICKET Playbook — Proprietary Methodology
White Rice's gamified behaviour change design toolkit — trained hundreds of UNICEF practitioners, cascading across Pakistan's provinces through master trainers. The 15-year book had no proprietary methodology to show. The 20-year book has one that is already multiplying itself.
New · 2025
World Bank PRMAC — 2,000 Villages
The largest single programme in White Rice's history — SBC strategy for 2,000 Punjab villages, 1,000 Agents of Change, a complete two-year behaviour change rollout plan. A programme at a scale that very few behaviour change organisations anywhere in the world can point to.
New · 2023
Walidain HumQadam — Pakistan's Largest Vision Canvas Exercise
550 vision canvases drawn by parents across five cities in a single programme — Pakistan's largest parental visioning exercise. Digital reach of 14.7M impressions. A programme that proved remote SBC delivery at national scale. A first.
Deepened · 2021–2026
Khawar's Legacy — Five More Years of Proof
The In Memoriam in the 15-year book was written in grief. The 20-year book writes it in legacy — what Khawar's vision produced in the five years since, what the company has become in his absence, what it means to carry a co-founder's dream and prove it right.
Reframe · 2026
From South Asian Agency to Global Methodology
Johns Hopkins CCP (250,000 workers globally, 4 languages, every continent). Spring Accelerator (4 countries). The 15-year book was proudly Pakistani. The 20-year book is Pakistani and global — and that tension is one of its richest themes. The space between origin and reach.
In Summary · The Concept

A book that lives
in the same space
as the work itself.

The Space Between is not a coffee table book about White Rice. It is a coffee table book about behaviour change — told through twenty years of White Rice's work, the communities they designed with, the partners who believed in them, and the human truth that knowledge alone has never been enough to change what people do. The reader closes the book having understood something essential about why people change — and why that understanding is the most valuable thing a development organisation can hold.

For a UNICEF director in Geneva, a World Bank programme manager in Washington, a health minister in Islamabad, or a young designer in Lahore who wants to do work that matters — this book should feel made for them. It is that rare object: a celebration that earns its ambition, not by proving that White Rice is good, but by making the reader understand why the work is necessary.
