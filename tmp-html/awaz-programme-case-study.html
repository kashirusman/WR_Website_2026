02

The Challenge

Pakistan's marginalised communities — particularly women and religious minority groups — face a compounding set of exclusions. They are under-represented in local government, absent from political decision-making, unconnected to media, and without formal channels to raise the community challenges they live with daily. Their issues — broken schools, absent waste collection, non-functional health facilities, unsafe public spaces — are known to them and invisible to everyone with the power to act.

"These communities were not voiceless. They had never been given a channel that reached the right ears. Awaz was about building that channel — and the confidence to use it."

For women specifically, the barriers are layered: social norms restrict mobility and public participation; low literacy limits access to written information; and a deep cultural belief that politics and public life are not spaces that belong to them. Changing this required not just information — it required a sustained behaviour change programme that built confidence, demonstrated possibility, and created visible proof that ordinary women from ordinary communities could become leaders.

The programme needed a creative partner who could operate across all of this simultaneously — designing grassroots tools for frontline workers, producing content that documented the movement's own progress, and building platforms that connected community voices to the decision-makers and media who could act on them.

03

The Strategy

White Rice's strategy for Awaz was built on a principle that runs through all our best work: communities are not audiences to be reached — they are agents to be activated. The creative and communication work was not designed to tell communities what to think or do. It was designed to show them what they were already capable of, and to give them the tools to act on it.

The strategy operated across four simultaneous registers: grassroots capacity-building through the community mobilisation toolkit; programme documentation through documentaries and animation; platform creation through the Awaz iReporter citizen journalism system; and media and advocacy linkage to ensure community voices reached the platforms that could create change.

Key messages were designed for different audiences, different platforms, and different stages of the behaviour change journey — from initial awareness and attitudinal shift, through confidence-building and skill development, to action, leadership, and sustained civic participation. Every piece of content, at every level, was designed to move someone one step further along that journey.

Awaz iReporter · The Platform That Changed Everything

When citizens become
the journalists of their
own lived reality.

The Awaz iReporter was the programme's most innovative and operationally significant product. White Rice trained and equipped community members — ordinary citizens, many of them women — to use their mobile phones to document the challenges in their communities: the school with no teacher, the road that floods every winter, the health facility that hasn't had medicines in months.

These video reports were fed into a central hub, curated, and shared with two critical audiences simultaneously — local politicians and government officials with the mandate to act, and media organisations with the platform to amplify. The result was a system that converted invisible grassroots frustration into visible, documented, attributable evidence that could not be ignored.

For the first time, communities had not just a voice — they had a microphone pointed in the right direction.

1
Training: Community members trained in mobile video documentation, story framing, and iReporter submission protocols
2
Documentation: Citizens film local challenges — facilities, infrastructure, services, governance failures — in their own words, from their own perspective
3
Central Hub: Reports submitted and curated — organised by issue, district, and urgency for escalation and media distribution
4
Escalation: Evidence shared with local politicians, government officials, and media organisations — creating accountability trails and story leads
5
Action & Amplification: Documented cases trigger government response, media coverage, and community advocacy — visible, evidence-based change

Women's Leadership · The Movement's Most Lasting Impact

Women who came to a workshop.
Women who went on to win elections.

Local Government Seats
Women from marginalised communities — who had never participated in public life before Awaz — secured seats in local government union councils. Not as quotas, but as credible, community-endorsed candidates who had earned the trust of their neighbours through years of visible leadership in the programme.
Political Party Membership
Several Awaz women went further — joining political parties as active members, not peripheral supporters. The programme created a pipeline of women with the confidence, skills, and community standing to operate in Pakistan's formal political system for the first time.
Community Voice & Advocacy
Most importantly, these women became the authentic voice of their marginalised communities on the issues that matter most — raising the concerns of religious minorities, advocating for basic service delivery, and giving visibility to the invisible in ways that formal civil society organisations often cannot.

The Impact

The most significant measure of Awaz's success is that it is still running. In development programming, a 10-year multi-phase programme is not a measure of spending — it is a measure of proof. Funders continue to invest, implementing partners continue to operate, and communities continue to participate because the evidence of change is visible and continuing. White Rice has been part of every phase of that evidence.

Citizens with a Voice
The iReporter platform created Pakistan's first structured citizen journalism system connecting community documentation to government action and media amplification.

The impact of Awaz cannot be captured in a single number. It lives in the woman who stood up in a union council meeting for the first time. In the school that was repaired because a community member filmed its broken roof and sent it to the right person. In the religious minority family that found out they had rights they had never been told about. These are the outcomes that don't appear in logframes. They are the ones that matter most.
